Coconut Curry Chickpeas: A Meat-Free Pot That Still Feels Rich

A single can of coconut milk, cut with water, stretches into a full pot of curry sauce without losing body. Canned chickpeas versus dry-soaked settles the true cost-per-serving winner.

INGREDIENT UNIT LEDGER

SERVINGS: 4
INGREDIENT ITEM QTY UNIT COST
Chickpeas (Cans) 2 cans (15oz) $1.80
Coconut Milk (Can) 1 can (13.5oz) $1.80
Yellow Onion, Diced 1 onion $0.30
Curry Powder 2 tbsp $0.30

Long-Grain Rice (for serving) 1.5 cups $0.52
ESTIMATED TOTAL MATRIX: $5.03
COST PER SERVING: $1.26

Preparation Protocol

  1. Build the Base: Cook onion and garlic in a pot over medium heat until softened, about 5 minutes.
  2. Bloom the Curry Powder: Stir in curry powder and cook for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  3. Simmer: Add drained chickpeas and coconut milk, cut with a splash of water if too thick. Simmer for 15-20 minutes until slightly thickened.
  4. Serve: Serve hot over freshly cooked rice.

Why the Numbers Work

Coconut milk and canned chickpeas are both priced at $1.80 here, together making up over 70% of the $5.03 total — but split across four servings, that's still just $1.26 per bowl for a dish that reads as rich and restaurant-style. The "cut with water" technique in the description matters more than it sounds: a full can of coconut milk stretched with a splash of water maintains body while extending the yield, which is the actual lever keeping this recipe affordable rather than any single cheap ingredient.

Storage & Leftovers

Refrigerate covered for up to 4 days; the coconut curry base holds up well and often tastes better the next day. Reheat gently on the stovetop or in the microwave, stirring in a small splash of water if it's thickened too much. Freezes well for up to 3 months — store the curry separately from the rice for the best texture on reheating.

Variations

  • Add vegetables: Frozen spinach, diced sweet potato, or cauliflower florets stretch the pot further and add minimal cost.
  • Spice it up: A pinch of cayenne or a spoonful of curry paste alongside the curry powder deepens the heat without changing the core cost.
  • Dry chickpeas: Soaking and boiling dry chickpeas instead of canned lowers cost further at the expense of extra prep time.

FAQ

Can I use light coconut milk to save money? You can, but it will produce a noticeably thinner sauce since most of the cost and richness comes from the coconut fat content.

Why bloom the curry powder in oil? Like most ground spice blends, curry powder contains fat-soluble aromatics that need a brief sizzle in hot fat to fully release — skipping this step leaves the flavor muted and raw-tasting.

Is canned or dry-soaked chickpeas actually cheaper? Dry beans are almost always cheaper per gram, but canned wins on total time — for a 20-minute recipe like this one, canned is the practical choice.
