Aglio e Olio: Maximizing Cheap Pantry Staples for Maximum Garlic Flavor

When the fridge is empty, Aglio e Olio saves the budget. Raw garlic slices infused into olive oil, finished with red pepper flakes, cost pennies per serving.

INGREDIENT UNIT LEDGER

SERVINGS: 4
INGREDIENT ITEM QTY UNIT COST
Spaghetti 12 oz $0.90
Garlic Cloves, Thinly Sliced 6 cloves $0.60
Olive Oil 5 tbsp $0.90
Red Pepper Flakes 1 tsp $0.06
Fresh Parsley, Chopped 2 tbsp $0.20
ESTIMATED TOTAL MATRIX: $2.66
COST PER SERVING: $0.67

Preparation Protocol

  1. Infuse the Oil: Warm olive oil in a skillet over low heat and add sliced garlic. Cook slowly for 4-5 minutes until golden, not browned.
  2. Boil the Pasta: Cook spaghetti in salted water until al dente. Reserve 1/2 cup pasta water before draining.
  3. Combine: Add red pepper flakes to the garlic oil, then toss in the drained pasta. Add pasta water as needed to loosen.
  4. Finish: Stir in fresh parsley just before serving.

Why the Numbers Work

Aglio e Olio wins the cost audit for one structural reason: it has no expensive load-bearing ingredient. Every other pasta sauce leans on protein, dairy, or produce that fluctuates hard with weekly grocery pricing — this one leans on a single garlic bulb and a bottle of oil that sits in the pantry for months. At $0.67 per serving, roughly 90% of the cost sits in the spaghetti itself, which means the "flavor" ingredients (garlic, chili, parsley) are essentially free riders. Buying garlic by the whole bulb instead of pre-peeled jars alone accounts for most of the savings here versus a store-bought jarred version.

Storage & Leftovers

Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Because there's no dairy or cream to break, this reheats cleanly in a skillet with a splash of water or olive oil over medium-low heat — a microwave works too, but the noodles firm up less pleasantly. It does not freeze well; the garlic-oil emulsion separates once thawed.

Variations

  • Add protein: A can of white beans or a fried egg on top turns this into a heartier meal for about $0.30-$0.50 more per serving.
  • Anchovy boost: Melt 2-3 anchovy fillets into the garlic oil for a savory depth that mimics a more expensive puttanesca, without changing the base cost much.
  • Breadcrumb topping: Toasted panko finished with a drizzle of oil adds crunch and stretches a half-empty bread bag into use.

FAQ

Why is my garlic bitter? It browned too fast. Keep the heat low and pull the pan off if the slices start going past golden — burnt garlic is the single most common way to ruin this dish.

Can I use garlic powder instead? You can in a pinch, but you lose the textural bite of the sliced cloves and most of the aromatic oil infusion that makes this dish work. If substituting, use about 1 tsp powder per 2 fresh cloves and add it directly to the warm oil.

Is bottled minced garlic cheaper? Usually not per equivalent clove — whole bulbs are almost always the better per-unit value, and they infuse the oil more evenly.
